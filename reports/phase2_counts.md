## Results (real data, 6,700 out-of-sample games 2021-10 to 2026-10; walk-forward, strictly earlier games only)
- **Totals have no demonstrable skill over the base-rate distribution.** Scored only after the regularisation was fixed (2024-06-30): log-loss difference to the base rate +0.0002 to +0.0005 per line, every interval spans zero (the model is, if anything, marginally worse). Whole-distribution RPS difference to the base rate -0.00001 (95% interval -0.00015 to +0.00013). The previous model (shootout goal excluded), scored against the totals as books settle them, is worse (log score -2.193 vs -2.179; RPS +0.00014).
- **The totals forecast is not calibrated as a distribution**: randomised PIT is far from uniform (lowest decile holds 503 games against 670 expected). The calibration slope on lines is 0.24 to 0.47 with a standard error of about 0.4, so it is too noisy to correct reliably.
- **The puck line has weak skill over its base rate** after the selection date: log-loss difference -0.0076 and -0.0062 (intervals reach to about zero). That is skill against a base rate, not against the market.
- **Count families**: shots need the negative binomial (out-of-sample NLL -0.0027 vs Poisson, dispersion 0.05); points, assists and goals are indistinguishable from Poisson (differences below 1e-4), so Poisson is the simpler family there.
- **Goalie saves**: the save-percentage prior kept choosing the largest value in the grid (200 to 12,800), meaning goalie identity adds no information to the model. The market is RESEARCH ONLY (it is also not priced live).

## Not done
- Empty-net goals are not modelled separately: the ingest does not store them. Their effect is absorbed in the dispersion, tie rate and the (imperfect) PIT; adding them needs play-by-play data.
- A bivariate-Poisson dependence term is not fitted; the diagonal tie correction is the only dependence.
